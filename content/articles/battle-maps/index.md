What makes a good battle map is largely dependent on the context it is used in. At it's core, battlemaps are meant to help players and DMs visualize the action that is taking place in the scene.

A couple of questions I like to ask when selecting battle maps for upcoming encounters:

**Does it help visualize the action?** Is the scene easy to understand at a glance? If players cannot intuit what is going on, they will not be able to make good choices.

**Does it introduce any other mechanics to combat?** Elevation, hazards, difficult terrain, lighting/darkness? A flat, empty battlefield can lead to flat, empty combat.

**Does it provide room for narrative?** Portals to be closed, crystals to be overloaded, a summoning stone for the enemies to be performing a dark ritual around.

This final issue of room for narrative, I can usually fix when running in a virtual tabletop like Roll20, which offers a variety of free/paid object packs which I can use to add special items and layer them on the map as appropriate. But if it comes built-in to the map, all the better.

## Making Battle Maps

Running multiple weekly games, I often source my battlemaps online. There are countless great map makers offering their works for free or cheaply on Patreon, Reddit, and more.

On the rare occasion I need to make something myself, I turn to these two tools.

- [Inkarnate](https://inkarnate.com) — A personal favorite.
- [Dungeondraft](https://dungeondraft.net/) - From the creators of Wonderdraft.
