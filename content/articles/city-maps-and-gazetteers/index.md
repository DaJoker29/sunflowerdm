I find city maps one of the more difficult maps to nail down, for a couple of reasons.

**Worldbuilding.** In my settting, each city is its own tapestry of culture, politics, annd history waiting to be explored. Each district has its own distinct character shaped by its population and demographics. All of this means a good amount of worldbuilding must be done before you even start making your city maps.

**Essential Services.** In my setting, cities serve as regional hubs that characters can return to between quests for typical services: food, rest, equipment, and upgrades. Some thought must be put in place ahead of time on how these services are fulfilled in each city.

**Gazetteer.** I find my city maps are fairly useless without a secondary document known as a gazetteer which functions as an index of the different districts and notable establishments within them.

Most of the work of city maps happens off the map. It's in figuring out where all of the important things in the city are. One reason I typically keep my city maps pretty simple and put most of my effort into my gazetteer.

{{< callout title="Layering History" >}}

One of my favorite tools for building a location's history is what I call layering. Inspired by Matt Colville, this technique is simply stacking different cultures on top of one another through time and seeing what comes out on the other side.

For example, let's say a group of reclusive wizards left a busy metropolis and founded a small hamlet to practice their arcane rituals in peace. The residual magic from their powerful rituals became infused with the land. Then, dwarven lumberjacks began migrating there to harvest magical timber from a nearby grove.

Centuries later, the city of Mystic Grove is world-renowned for producing the finest wands, staves, and spell paper in the land. Tourists flock to see the enchanted tools levitating at the Sorcerer's Mill. The kegs and taps at the local tavern automagically replenish from ensorceled barrels.

Doesn't that sound way cooler than "a dwarven city" or "a town of mages".
