I find city maps one of the most difficult to nail down, for a few reasons.

**Worldbuilding.** Each city is its own tapestry of culture, politics, annd history waiting to be explored. Each district has its own distinct character shaped by its population and demographics. All of this means a good amount of worldbuilding must be done before you even start making the city maps.

**Essential Services.** When cities serve as regional hubs that characters can return to between quests for typical services: food, rest, equipment, and upgrades. Some thought must be put in place ahead of time on how these services are fulfilled in each city. If the party arrives in the middle of the night and want a meal, which places would be open and how would they find it?

**Gazetteer.** City maps are fairly useless without a secondary document known as a gazetteer which functions as an index of the different districts and notable establishments within them. Without this secondary document, the maps itself has limited utility.

Most of the work of city maps happens off the map. It's in figuring out where all of the important things in the city are. One reason I typically **keep city maps simple and put the most effort into my gazetteers**.

{{< callout title="Layering History" >}}

One of my favorite tools for building a location's history is what I call layering. Inspired by Matt Colville, this technique is simply stacking different cultures on top of one another through time and seeing what comes out on the other side.

For example, let's say a group of reclusive wizards left a busy metropolis and founded a small hamlet to practice their arcane rituals in peace. The residual magic from their powerful rituals became infused with the land. Then, dwarven lumberjacks began migrating there to harvest magical timber from a nearby grove.

Centuries later, the city of Mystic Grove is world-renowned for producing the finest wands, staves, and spell paper in the land. Tourists flock to see the enchanted tools levitating at the Sorcerer's Mill. The kegs and taps at the local tavern automagically replenish from ensorceled barrels.

Doesn't that sound way cooler than "a dwarven city" or "a town of mages".

{{</callout>}}

## Making City Maps

{{< figure src="solara-map.png" alt="Map of Solara" caption="Created with MFCG (Procgen Arcana)" >}}

While many of the tools I've previous recommended would work fantastically for city maps, I find [Watabou's Medieval Fantasy City Generator](https://watabou.github.io/city.html) is my go-to. I go through a few iterations until I find a shape and layout that I like, then I start renaming the districts to incorporate the lore established in the worldbuilding.

## Making Gazetteers

This is where the brunt of the work goes, because one you have the districts figured out, then you must go figure out what goes into each of them. At a minimum, I like to keep a list of the key establishments divided by services provided. When I have more time, I'll go through and decide on which district each establishment belongs to so when I'm describing the districts or if the players start investigating, I can reference what locations they might pass by.
